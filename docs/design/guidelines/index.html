01 · Principles

The planning is the interface.

Timon is open all day on a dispatcher's screen. Everything around the planning stays quiet, so that colour on screen always means something.

1

Show the trade

A row is a real driver with his tractor, his trailer and his deadlines. Not a calendar slot.

2

Never miss a conflict

A conflict is drawn, named, and always comes with a solution and its cost.

3

Dense, not cramped

Thirty resources on one screen, readable at a glance. Figures line up.

4

One accent at a time

Petrol acts. Brass marks the time. Nothing else competes for attention.

02 · Name and logo

The ring that
holds it together.

In French, a timon is the drawbar that ties a team to its wagon. The logo keeps the one part that holds everything: the coupling ring, set in brass in place of the o.

At small sizes the ring takes back its drawbar.
That is the icon: favicon, app tile, avatar.

Timon · Brand guidelines07
07 · Signature elements

What makes a screen look like Timon.

Unit links

Tractor and trailer joined by a short bar, like the drawbar itself. A missing driver is a dashed link.

Brass time line and load curve

Now is always brass. The curve shows how busy each two hours are; nights shrink to a quarter.

Two-lane conflict

Two orders that overlap split the row over a hatched area. The problem is visible before it is read.

Costed solutions

Each way out of a conflict states its price: empty kilometres, a missed slot, a subcontractor's rate.
